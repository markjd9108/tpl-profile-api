One Way
Effective practice

Effective teams agree the expected result in plain words before work changes hands, then ask the receiver to say it back. The Clarity Loop: Clarify and Confirm.

What it means

People are thorough at the end of a task and weak at the start of it. The missing step is agreeing the expected result and the priorities before the work changes hands.

What it costs

Repeat visits, rework between Sales and Service, and customers who hear two versions of the same promise.

Do this week
Close every handover with the expected result

End each handover with two sentences: what the customer expects, and by when. The receiver says it back in their own words.

Ask in your one-to-ones

“In your last handover, what did you and the receiver agree the finished result would look like?”

Two Hands
Effective practice

Effective teams share what they know early, even when it seems obvious, and agree how separate pieces of work fit before they join. The Collaboration Reset: Share and Sync.

What it means

People help each other readily once a problem is visible. They rarely share what they know before it is needed, so other teams plan on old information.

What it costs

Late information, missed commitments to customers and time lost correcting plans.

Do this week
Send a weekly three-line update across teams

Each people manager sends the teams that depend on them three lines each week: what changed, what is coming, and what help is needed.

Ask in your one-to-ones

“Which team was affected by one of your changes this month, and when did they find out?”

Risk Assessment
Effective practice

Effective teams agree what a decision must achieve, decide within clear limits, and set a date to review it. The Decision Engine: Decide.

What it means

People act well once a decision is made. They are uncomfortable owning a decision when information is incomplete, so passing it upward feels safer.

What it costs

Slow decisions, overloaded managers and a team that waits instead of acting.

Do this week
Agree a written decision scope with each people manager

Write down which decisions each person owns, the limits, and when to involve you. Within that scope, they decide and tell you after.

Ask in your one-to-ones

“Which decision did you pass up this week that was yours to make?”

07 · How we measure

Where these results come from

1

Hands-on simulations

Small groups complete short, timed simulations that put each skill under pressure. Everyone takes turns giving instructions, carrying them out and observing.

2

Situational questions

After each simulation, people choose what they did from four realistic options. Each option is scored against published research on effective teamwork.

3

Two views of every person

Teammates answer the same questions about each other. Own and teammate answers are blended, so no single view decides a result.

The four levels
StrongRight almost every time, including under pressure.
DevelopingRight most of the time. Less reliable under pressure.
EmergingRight some of the time. Likely to cause mistakes and rework.
FoundationRarely right yet. A clear gap to close.
Why you can trust it
  • A proven format. Situational judgement tests are among the most studied workplace assessments, with links to job performance and teamwork.
  • Behaviour, not opinion. People report what they did minutes earlier, not how they see themselves in general.
  • One standard for everyone. Every answer is scored by the same fixed rules.
The simulations behind this report
Communication
The Clarity Loop: Clarify, Check, Confirm
Two WordsOne person may only answer yes or no while the other describes the work. Tests how precisely people describe a task.
One WayInstructions flow one way for the first half, then questions are allowed. Tests how quickly people check understanding.
Ambiguity & AssumptionNeither person can see the work. Tests whether people test their assumptions before they agree.
Decision Making
The Decision Engine: Define, Discover, Decide
Risk AssessmentA pair chooses between two options with little information, then commits. Tests how people compare options and commit.
Choose Your ConditionsTeams choose their working conditions before they see the task. Tests what people trade off and how they react once the choice is fixed.
Locked InEvery step is final once taken. Tests careful decisions and recovery after a mistake that cannot be undone.
Collaboration
The Collaboration Reset: Share, Sync, Support
Split KitEach person holds part of the information and nobody can see the rest. Tests whether people share what only they know.
Two HalvesTwo people build separate parts that must join at the end. Tests how people agree how the parts fit.
Two HandsTwo people share control of every move. Tests how people support each other in close joint work.

Research: Christian, Edwards and Bradley (2010); Webster et al. (2020). Full method at theperformancelens.com/methodology
